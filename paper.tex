\title{Integrating Data Migration with Replication in Distributed Key-Value Stores}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Author list omitted for double-blind reviewing}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Elastically scaling distributed key-value stores (redistributing key-value shards in an online manner) is an important way to gracefully absorb load variations in modern data centers, and rapid data migration is a key enabler of elasticity.
While several distributed key-value stores (KVSs) have targeted rapid data migration, none so far have investigated the efficent integration of data migration and replication mechanisms, nor have they studied fault-tolerance aspects of the data migration process (i.e., its robustness under failures).
%in non-replicated systems, migration performance is impacted when considering replicated setups, 
%leading to reduced migration throughput and higher duration. 
In this paper we present \OurImplementation{}, 
a distributed KVS that integrates the data migration and replication protocols, using a variant of chain-replication for efficient pipelined bulk RDMA transfers, while migration control actions follow the standard replication protocol.
\OurImplementation{} schedules data migrations as transactions, namely as an atomic actions that are also robust to crash failures.
Our experiments demonstrate that replication leaves the duration of a migration unchanged at three replicas per shard group, and is paid in steady-state throughput instead.
In contrast, competing systems (e.g., RAMCloud variants such as Rocksteady) are significantly impacted when migrating data in replicated settings.
Under crash failures, \OurImplementation{} can recover a migration transaction in less than 3.4 seconds and complete it at most 3 seconds slower than failure-free migration.
\end{abstract}
